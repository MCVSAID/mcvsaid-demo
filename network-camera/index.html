Add a network camera

A camera that is not a phone, such as a PTZ camera on the network. OBS takes its picture over NDI; the manager knows it by a name, a slot and the NDI source it sends, and the director chooses it like any other camera.

The camera
Its NDI source
As NDI names it: the sending device, then the source in brackets. Choose one found above, or type it exactly as OBS lists it in an NDI source’s “Source name”.

Network cameras here

    What it needs

    • In OBS: the NDI runtime and the DistroAV plugin (both free; installed by an administrator of this computer, then OBS restarted). Without them OBS cannot take NDI: the camera can still be added here, and says it is not ready, and why, until they are there.
    • The camera on the same network as this computer, with NDI switched on in its own settings.

    After adding it

    1. Press Refresh OBS scenes on the Show page: it makes the camera’s NDI source in OBS, in its lowest-delay mode, with its full-screen scene and its split screens.
    2. Its card on the Cameras page says whether OBS shows its picture. Once it does, the director can cut to it.
    3. Its delay is set for you. It has no timing strip to be measured by, so with OBS sync on the manager watches its picture beside the phones’ and, once the same things have been seen to happen in both, lines it up with them and keeps it so. Its card (Maintenance) says when it was last timed; the delay can be set there by hand as a starting point.

    A PTZ camera is aimed and zoomed on the Operate page once its card has its control address; its picture size, frame rate and exposure are set on the camera itself. A network camera has no battery, temperature, lens, recording or app for the manager to look after, so none of those is shown for it.
