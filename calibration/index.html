Calibrate

Once the cameras are up and streaming, one person can calibrate them from the stage: open the calibrate page on a phone, log in, and hold it up where the cameras see it. Pre-show calibration check offers the stage beacon first when one stands ready (a camera it settles is not walked to), then takes them from camera to camera, each visited once and measured with its own picture on air and its own microphone alone; then the cameras’ colours when they are due, matched by what the cameras see; then asks where the programme’s sound should come from, and with the programme mix measures the mix’s own timing (from the stage beacon when one stands ready and can be sure of it, else with the flashes at the camera that leads the mix); Calibrate sound flashes and beeps while the manager measures each camera’s lip sync; Calibrate colour shows a chart that the manager finds in every camera by itself, fits each camera’s colours and puts the corrections in OBS; with a network camera, Time the network cameras flashes while the manager times it against the phones by the flashes both see, and sets what it is held by in OBS.

Before the show

Are the sound and the cameras lined up? Gathered from what the manager already knows; nothing is measured by looking here, and it keeps itself up to date.

Stage beacon

A phone left on a stand in the stage area, its screen towards the cameras, with the camera app set to Stage beacon. Its screen shows two dim halves that shade into each other and it plays a soft hiss, both coded, for about 20 seconds; each camera's own stream says how far its sound is from its picture. Nothing goes on air, nothing is muted and OBS records nothing, so it can be run during the show. One kind of run is the exception, and is for before the show only: timing the programme mix (Set the mix’s timing from the beacon, and Set lip sync from the beacon while Programme sound is the programme mix) puts the camera that leads the mix on air and has OBS record the programme for about half a minute, that camera’s microphone alone in the mix and every other sound in OBS muted meanwhile, then puts all of it back; it is refused while OBS records or streams. A phone with no lip sync gets one from its first reading, and the Before the show list says how sure that is; one it is not sure of stays due there until it is measured through OBS (the Pre-show calibration check, or the room’s sound). After that the beacon checks each camera keeps where it was set. The check on the phone offers the beacon as its first step, and walks only to the cameras the beacon leaves. A network camera is timed against the phones by the same light, as OBS shows it from each: with every run, and by itself every so often with next to no hiss.
